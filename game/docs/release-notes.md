# Release notes

Notes for the next release, to copy into the GitHub release (RELEASING.md step 2).

## Unreleased

- **VIP shuttle rings stay on the shuttle's icon** at every zoom (they used a different position from the icon: off the ship and jittery zoomed out).
- **Waypoint and known-location icons shrink and fade as you zoom out** across the galaxy view (to 70 % size and 60 % opacity fully zoomed out).
- **No more herd migration notifications** (The Rim fauna): the herds still migrate, without the news line and warning.
- **The Founders' Compact seal is much fainter**, and zoomed out to the galaxy a threat marker on a world sits on its system's star instead of off to one side.
- **Game Editor: finish a Great Work.** A "Finish" button for every Great Work under construction completes it at once through its normal completion (for the Beacon: the countdown to the first beam starts).
- **A Watcher stays a mystery until the war.** Before the Beacon's first beam a Watcher showing itself over a world is still an "Unidentified contact" in the selection panel, lists and orders (no name, empire or stats); once the war begins it is known.
- **Weapon range circles are on by default** (Settings). Players who had them off by the old default get them on once; switching them off again sticks.
- **Work in progress add-ons sit in a closed dropdown, apart from the expansion switches.** The new-game add-ons list ends with a "Work in progress (N)" dropdown that is closed every time the page opens (the header also counts how many inside are ticked). An expansion's one-click switch no longer ticks or unticks its work-in-progress members, and an expansion made only of them (The Rim, Living Galaxy) has no switch; those add-ons are ticked one by one inside the dropdown. Add-ons that another add-on needs are still pulled in as before. Saved choices and old saves are unchanged.
- **Fix: crash merging maps with a destroyed empire.** A long game could throw in the map merge when a diplomatic relation changed with an empire that had since been destroyed (its resource map is cleared by the teardown). The merge now skips an empire without its maps, as the original does.
- **Holdouts: the AI weighs the defence bases too.** An AI fleet goes for a precursor holdout's world only when it is clearly stronger (125 %, holdoutAiRatioPct) than the holdout's home fleet and its defence bases together.
- **Stranded Watchers show foundries you never found (Harvest Cycle).** The Long Night now records every Reclaimer foundry still standing, not only the ones you located; boarding a stranded Watcher in Age 2 reveals one of those you never found (then the Truth of the ledger). The Knowledge page still lists only what you knew.
- **Biofuel depots in the war (Giantworks Beacon).** The AI coalition now strikes Reclaimer biofuel depots it can see (the lightest priority, after every other operation; gather-then-strike, only with a force twice the depot, its guard and the warships near it, at most two at once; params beaconAiDepotOn / RatioPct / Max), and the Diet's war credit counts each depot destroyed (10 points, beaconWarCreditDepot).
- **Age 2 survivor arks and stranded Watchers (Harvest Cycle).** In Age 2 the Generation Ships arc runs even if that add-on was not picked: the world ships are survivors of the old age, they come once the empires reach tech 2 with an escort of 1.5x the target's firepower, carry Age 1 level-1 tech (shields always included; the empire an ark founds keeps it), and meeting an ark's scout is the fifth knowledge milestone. One to three damaged Watchers also lie stranded and cloaked at Harvest Scars: they cannot jump, the AI knows where they are and keeps its colony ships and fleets away, but your ships that come near make one decloak and open fire; it cloaks again once you leave. Boarding one (very hard) gives you the ship and shows a foundry (or a Truth), and left alone for 40 years it leaves to join the next Reclamation. Once found, it is listed on the Knowledge page under "Left behind".
- **War credit in the Galactic Diet (Giantworks Beacon + the Diet on).** Once the Reclaimers are at war, every empire keeps a share-of-the-war tally: points for Reclaimer warships destroyed (by strength), capital ships destroyed or captured, Watchers killed or captured, harvesters and foundries destroyed, harvest cores retaken, people freed (cattle ships boarded, pens opened), refugees brought safely to a world by relocation transports, and cattle ships killed. Once a year the Diet reviews its members' tallies: the top three contributors (at least 10% of the total) gain Diet vote weight for the year (+15%, +10%, +5%) and standing with every other member (reputation: +4, +2, +1 toward them); a member whose share of the work is under 30% of its share of the members' fleets (and that holds at least 5% of them) loses 5% vote weight and standing (-2 with each member), and a news line names the leader. The tally shows on the Knowledge page ("Share of the war", the empires you have met) and as a "War credit" line on the Diet screen; AI members that lag may commit 25% more of their fleet to the coalition's operations. - **Age 2 knowledge milestones 2-8 (Harvest Cycle).** Your first hyperjump ("the songs were true"), the first contact with another species (each species tells the myth its own way; meeting three different ones unlocks the Truth "the shared myth"), the first old-capital ruin surveyed ("we were not the first"), the first Harvest Scar your ships find ("the pens"), the first Founders' Compact rumour of the new age ("it is happening again", only if you remember Age 1) and the Galactic Diet re-forming ("the preparation begins") each open a popup once, and every one you reach is listed on the Knowledge page under "The New Age". AI empires reach them too, without popups. If you remember Age 1, contact, ruins and scars end with "You know what this is." The survivor-ark milestone has its hook but waits for the arks. The texts are placeholders.
- **Precursor holdouts (Age 2, Harvest Cycle).** The new galaxy holds 2 to 5 small empires of the age before (by galaxy size), each a core world and 2 or 3 colonies within 2 sectors, in quiet regions clear of the homes, the Harvest Scars and the old Beacon site. They have shields and old reactors and beams (at least tech level 4, 3 above the best ordinary empire), garrisons and defence bases, and the stock AI runs them for defence only: they never colonise, never declare war and sign no treaties, so conquest is the only way to their tech. The core is shielded from invasion (a landing is wiped out and the garrison untouched) until every colony is taken; then a message says the shield has fallen. Each colony has a 20% chance (`holdoutRiggedPct`) of being rigged: it is destroyed with the planet-destroyer explosion a day after it is taken. The warning: when an armed ship of an empire at war with them arrives over a rigged colony, evacuation freighters leave it and a message says the colonists are fleeing. Conquering the core gives the conqueror every technology the holdout had. The Intelligence screen's Knowledge page lists the holdouts you have found, with the shield state. All placeholder texts (TODO-PROSE).
- **Precursor holdouts keep a home fleet and stay out of the Diet and the victory race (Age 2, Harvest Cycle).** Each holdout starts with 15 to 20 warships (cruisers, destroyers, frigates and escorts of its own shielded, frozen-tech designs; options `holdoutFleetMin` / `holdoutFleetMax`) spread over its core and colonies, patrolling only its own worlds in turn (they never leave its systems, and they fight anyone who attacks as ordinary ships do). While the core's shield stands, one lost ship is rebuilt free at the core every 90 to 120 days (`holdoutRebuildMin` / `holdoutRebuildMax`) up to the starting size; once the shield drops the rebuilding stops for good. AI empires attack a holdout's world only with a fleet at least as strong as its whole home fleet (`holdoutAiRatioPct`). The holdouts are not members of the Galactic Diet (no seat, vote or dues) and do not count in the stock victory conditions; they stay ordinary for contact. The Knowledge page shows a scouted holdout's fleet size roughly.
- **Simplified:** one evacuation (the Diet's order places ordinary relocation orders; its migration boosts, growth cut and revenue rule are gone), Reclaimer foundry intel only reports strength (no tech piece), the Severance's blockade is the Great Work's (a holder shoots arriving freighters, even with Blockades That Bite off), and dead empires' mega-station pieces are recorded only by Age 2's ruins.
- **The Reclaimers store their fleet biofuel in depots (Giantworks Beacon).** Visible Reclaimer bases, lightly guarded, hold the fuel that pays their warships, cattle ships and response fleets: raised round each harvest core (in its own system) and, on about one conquest in six, at a world they take, one every 15 days up to a cap that grows with the galaxy (10 up to 250 star systems, 20 at 1,000, 40 at 4,000). The stock is shared equally between the living depots, and destroying one burns exactly its share of what the Reclaimers hold at that moment (you are told how much). With no depot left, new fuel cannot be stored: it is spent within days or lost. Destroyed depots are rebuilt slowly (one per 180 days, paid from the fuel) and never above the cap; the response fleets defend a threatened depot as they would a threatened held world. Depots you have seen are listed on the Knowledge page (click to centre). They do not carry over into the next cycle.
- **Relocate population; Reclaimer refugee interceptors (with the Giantworks Beacon on).** On the colony screen's Population tab, a new block lets you move people from one of your colonies to another: pick the destination, the amount (slider or field, in millions) and press Relocate. It costs $1M per 10 billion people (paid at once from the state treasury), takes a small approval hit at both worlds for two years, and only races that may live at the destination move, up to its free room; a full evacuation abandons the colony (with a warning). Only real private passenger ships carry the people (idle warships escort a laden one), packed in at ten times their normal passenger capacity on these runs only; each trip has a 10% chance of losing 20% of its people to disease and starvation. The block shows the progress ("3.2 of 10 billion moved"). The receiving world earns income x (1 - 0.5 x min(1, people taken in / its population before them)) for 10 years after the last arrivals (refreshed, not stacked); the Diet's evacuation intake now uses the same scaled rule (it was a flat 50% for 2 years above an 8% intake; the duration is unchanged). AI empires use the order only for colonies that enemy warships, a blockade or the Reclaimers' wave threaten. Once the Reclamation is at war, the Reclaimers also field small interceptor groups (a cruiser with assault pods, a destroyer and two frigates; more groups as they hold more worlds, paid from the fleet biofuel) that patrol their worlds, raid private stations when idle, and drop everything to chase any relocation transport they can detect within 6 sectors of their space: the cruiser can drag it out of warp, boards and seizes it, and its people go to the nearest harvest core's pens unless the ship is retaken first (a transport destroyed instead loses them but for escape pods).
- **Age 2 rebuilds only the survivors, and the Long Night works in multiplayer; advisors warn at half the people gone (Harvest Cycle).** Of Age 1's empires only those still owning a colony when the Long Night begins return in Age 2 (pre-warp, same name, race, colours and flag), each on its last world: its capital if it still held it, else its most populous colony, reset to a normal pre-warp home (two in one system: one moves to its next-best world). The rest of the galaxy is filled with new pre-warp empires placed at random up to the original empire count (all survivors are kept if more survived), keeping clear of the survivors' systems, the Harvest Scars and the old Beacon site. You start on your capital if it survived, else on your surviving colony closest to the galaxy's centre, else on your Age 1 home world with your old identity. The homes are chosen before the pre-warp home systems are set up, so the crashed hull lies in each real home system. In Age 1, when the Beacon has processed half of the galaxy's people, each human player gets the pop-up "A World Beyond Their Reach" once (settle a world at the far edge), and AI empires lean up to 60% more toward colony targets at the rim. In a multiplayer game the host builds the Age 2 galaxy and sends it to every client as a resync (the clients do not regenerate it); a multiplayer client on the in-thread loop (Settings: sim worker off) cannot build it and is brought over by the same resync. The state digest now hashes every NaN alike (a NaN computed in a pre-warp game and the same NaN read back from a save differed, which would have made every resync of an Age 2 game fail).
- **AI empires at war with the Reclaimers design their new warships to get through the Reclaimers' plates (Giantworks Beacon).** The Reclaimers' armour cancels every hit at or under its reactive rating, and beam damage falls with range, so fleets of ordinary beams mostly bounced off. Now each AI empire at war with the Reclaimers scores every weapon type it has researched by the damage a hit does past the plates at the expected engagement range (whatever components the age's research tree offers, none named), and its new warship designs swap the weak weapons for the best type, within the hull's size limit; a design review is forced when the war begins (and every two years after), and Smarter AI's retrofit then brings the old ships up to the new design. Nothing changes without the Beacon, for your own empire, or at peace with the Reclaimers.
- **Age 2 (the Preparation) world: the old capitals are ruins, and Reclaimer hulks lie about (Harvest Cycle).** The capitals of the biggest Age 1 empires (up to 8, by colonies held when the Long Night began, and any empire that died holding mega-station parts) become stock ruins in the new galaxy, on the same world or, where a survivor's home system is in the way, the nearest free rocky world; the stock survey pays money and research progress, and the first empire to survey the ruin of an empire that died holding mega-station parts gets those parts, once (a human is told in a popup). A few damaged Reclaimer warship hulks (about a third of the square root of the star count, at least 2, at most 10) are mixed into the abandoned ships near the old harvest scars and capitals, in the Reclaimer fleet art; the stock abandoned-ship investigation claims one (a quarter are a pirate ambush, as for any stock derelict) and repairs are costly. In the system of each capital that fell to the Reclaimers or was destroyed in the Beacon war (the first five by date) lie 2-4 more hulks, the story of the battle: at most one claimable, the rest broken beyond repair and still booby-trapped (the stock abandoned-ship explosion). - **The mega-station can be built (the Diet vote, Age 1 and across the Long Night).** With the finished blueprint a human player can move "Build the Severance" (a placeholder name) in the Galactic Diet and pick the place: the Diet window closes, you click the map and the place snaps to the nearest empty void (one per galaxy; AIs cannot move it). When the vote passes, you and every member that voted yes and stands well with you hand over the parts they hold; the rest keep theirs (a captured capital still moves them). The pooled parts cap the progress: beam lens 20%, link conduit 15%, cloak lattice 15%, mind fragments 25% (of 30) and void frames 25% (of 4); at the cap it waits (no decay) and the pledged empires hand over whatever they win later. The structure is a Founders' Compact base (the Great Work scaffold, no new art) at the place, unarmed and indestructible; each contributor sends up to 20% of its private freighters to it (an idle freighter is redirected there, delivers, then is the private sector's again), so trade at home is dented. It takes 60-70 years of ACTIVE construction (a delivery in the last year; time stalled for parts does not count). A hostile armed force (pirates, the Reclaimers, an empire at war with a contributor) that holds its orbit shoots the arriving freighters (the same blockade as the Great Work's, even with Blockades That Bite off; a freighter shot down counts as sent but not arrived); freighters sent with none arriving for 2 years cost it 1% per 2 years from then on. Across the Long Night the half-built structure stays at the same place (dormant: it must be found by a ship and a new Diet vote, "Resume the Severance", continues it), every empire keeps its pieces by name, and a dead empire's pieces lie in Age 2's ruins. At 100% it fires (a popup) and the game is won. The Harvest Cycle option now needs the Galactic Diet (the add-on picker switches Characters & Victory and its Diet on with it). The Knowledge page's "Mega-station" section data (who holds each piece, the blueprint, the structure's progress, cap and status) is ready (`megaKnowledgeRows`). Player-facing texts are TODO-PROSE.
- **The Beacon's construction waits for the galaxy's technology.** The Beacon's Great Work now builds at full speed only up to a stage that follows the tech of the leading empires (the mean of the highest researched tech level of the top half of the empires, rounded up in count): below tech 2 up to 20% of the work, tech 2 40%, tech 3 60%, tech 4 80%, tech 5 100%. Past that stage it builds at 25% of its speed until the galaxy catches up, so a galaxy that is not ready cannot be reclaimed early. While it is held back, the Great Work's label and its Intelligence status say "Progress slowed: it waits for something" (without saying what it is), and the Galactopedia article describes it neutrally. The siphon gate stays, but the baseline siphon of 80 knowledge a month at the gate is now off by default (the stages prevent the stall it was added for). Options: `beaconTechCap1` to `beaconTechCap5`, `beaconTechSlowPct`, `beaconSiphonBaseline` (default 0).
- **The Beacon is a fake-out ending: the Reclamation goes on, with a new victory and the first part of the mega-station path (Age 1).** Destroying the Beacon (after it has fired) no longer ends the game. The Reclaimers keep their fleets, harvest cores, Watchers and seasons: with the Beacon gone their ships are made at, and gather round, their biggest held world (the Beacon's site if they hold none), later seasons prepare and strike from staging points as before (no new mega beam), raids and response fleets go on. The game is now won by destroying 95% of the Reclaimers' assets (ships and bases by strength, held worlds counted as ten average ships each, against their peak; option `beaconAssetVictoryPct`), shown as "Reclaimer assets left: N% of their peak" in the Intelligence status of the Beacon, the Watchers and the cores, or (later) by firing the mega-station. The Galactic Diet (with the Diet on) presents destroying the Beacon as the way to end the Reclaimers. The empire whose ship destroys the Beacon after it fired gets the beam lens and a vague warning. New parts, kept per empire in the save: the beam lens (the Beacon), the link conduit (a harvester destroyed), the cloak lattice (a Watcher destroyed), mind fragments (Reclaimer capital ships destroyed, 30 needed) and void frames (foundries, 4 needed; foundries come later); the empire whose ship made the kill gets the credit. Parts can be traded between empires on good terms (a new "Mega-station parts" group on the trade screen; an AI with a bad attitude or reputation toward you, or at war, refuses to give or take them) and go to the captor with a captured capital. One year after the first mega beam every empire learns the place of a precursor world adrift between systems (a map marker); each empire whose ship reaches it gets the mega-station blueprint, a research project of 5,000,000 points that takes 25% of that empire's research output while it runs (AIs send a ship some weeks after the reveal and race for it). Placeholder texts for the new messages are marked TODO-PROSE. Options: `beaconAssetVictoryPct`, `beaconAssetWorldShips`, `beaconAssetMinPeakShips`, `beaconRevealDays`, `beaconBlueprintCost`, `beaconBlueprintSharePct`, `beaconPlanetReach`, `beaconMindFragmentsNeeded`, `beaconVoidFramesNeeded`, `beaconPartTradeAttitude`, `beaconPartTradeValue`.
- **The Reclaimers build their warships in hidden foundries (Age 1 part).** When the Beacon is finished the Reclaimers raise foundries: real bases in the empty void between systems (5 in a galaxy of up to 250 star systems, 10 at 1,000, 20 at 4,000, never replaced), cloaked from every sensor, each with a small cloaked guard that mends itself fast. From the second season on, new Reclaimer warships (strike waves, the Beacon's guard, response fleets, raiders) are made at a living foundry and fly out; what the foundries can build per pass is the share that is still alive (none left, no new ships); the first beam's arrivals are the stockpile and stay as they were. A Reclaimer capital ship is now very hard to board (boarding defence at least 2,500) and a strike group's capital with no escort falls back to the Beacon; capture one and hold it for 5 days without it being retaken or destroyed and its mind core reveals one foundry to you (an AI empire that does it learns one too). A revealed foundry shows in the Intelligence screen's Threats & Leads and can be investigated by an agent: it reports the foundry's strength (its guard and output). A ship that comes within reach of a living foundry cannot hyperjump away until it is destroyed. Each foundry you destroy is credited to you (for the void frames).
- **A Knowledge page in the Intelligence screen (with the Great Works).** A new Knowledge button beside Threats opens a page of what your empire has found about the Reclaimers and the Great Works, and nothing it has not: the war at a glance (phase, days to the next launch, share of the galaxy's people eliminated, people taken and freed, worlds conquered and retaken), the Beacon, the harvest cores and harvesters you have learned of, known staging points, corrupted worlds you have identified, the Watchers (with where your sensors last detected a cloaked one, fading with age), the Founders' Compact's founding worlds and the Great Work itself, and short recommended moves that unlock with what you know. Click a row to centre the map on it; a link opens the Galactopedia article. Empty sections for the mega-station, foundries, sleeper warnings and the Age 2 memory record are ready for those features.
- **The Diet can order the evacuation of the worlds near the Beacon; Diet votes weigh who proposed.** With the Giantworks Beacon and the Diet on, any member (you included) can move "Evacuate the worlds near the Great Work" while the Great Work is being built or before the Beacon's first beam. When it passes, each member places relocation orders (the relocation order's plan, cost, transports and income loss) for its own colonies within four sectors of it, the densest first, to its nearest safer colony with room. AI members that fear the Beacon propose and back it; the owners of the threatened worlds vote against. All Diet votes now also weigh the proposer: an AI member leans toward a motion from an empire it likes or respects (opinion, plus the reputation ledger) and against one from an empire it dislikes (up to about 30 points either way), and the adviser line on a Diet motion says so. Tunable in the Giantworks parameters (beaconEvac*).
- **Blockades strangle the Great Work, and blockades shoot (with Blockades That Bite on).** A blockaded founding world, or a wonder's extra supplier (the Beacon's corrupted worlds), sends no convoys. Each founding world the empires control (blockaded by any empire, or owned by one) cuts the Great Work's build speed by 20%, at most 60%: with all three founders controlled it builds at 40% of its speed from what the remaining suppliers send; a blockaded corrupted world also adds nothing to the Beacon's build speed. You can now declare a blockade (a Blockade order) of an independent founding world or supplier without a war; it holds while your armed ships keep the orbit (two daily checks in a row), and the Compact's anger and standing follow as for an attack on its convoys. The Great Work's label and Intelligence status show the build speed, and the players get messages when a supplier is blockaded ("The convoys from X have stopped") and when it is lifted. Blockades now shoot in general: the holder's armed ships in orbit attack the ships of the blockaded side that arrive or leave (any ship of the owner within 6,000 units; at peace only unarmed ones) and, for a supplier world, the Compact's convoy freighters, including the ones it spawns for free, with ordinary Attack orders (two ships to a target). New options: `giantworksBlockadeCutPct`, `giantworksBlockadeMaxCutPct`, `blockadeShoots`, `blockadeShootRange`, `blockadeShooters`.
- **The Beacon's site defends itself before the first mega beam.** The Great Work's scaffold now carries a strong shield (25,000) from its first stage. Any attack on the scaffold, or on the finished Beacon before its first beam, makes the Watchers appear within 1,000 units of it at once and fire on every empire's ship in that system (uncloaked), then go back to what they were doing and cloak again; pirates and monsters only draw them if they attack the site. If the shield is broken, a large Reclaimer fleet of the Watchers' faction (150% of the strongest attacking force there, built free) arrives and holds the site until the Beacon is built and its first beam fires, then joins its guard. No war is declared by any of this: war still begins at the beam. New messages tell you when the Watchers come and when the shield fails; settings in the Giantworks add-on options.
- **Giantworks: two Great Works by default, the first always the Beacon; founders are re-chosen at the Gathering when they are gone.** The automatic number of Great Works is now 2 in every galaxy (the >1400-star rule is gone; the option still takes 1-3), and "Guarantee the Beacon" is on by default, so the first work is the Beacon whatever the number of empires, and the second is rolled from the other enabled wonders (never the same founders or the same site). In a late-age start the independent worlds chosen as founders at game start had mostly been absorbed by the empires within 8 years, so the Gathering found "no founders left" and the work was abandoned; now, if fewer than two founding circles still have a free independent world when the Gathering comes, the founders are chosen again from the independents that remain (a pilgrim colony for a region without), by the same rules as at the start.
- **Reclaimer capital ships run down fleeing enemies.** An enemy ship or fleet (yours or an AI's) that fought a Reclaimer capital's fleet and then hyperjumps away is followed into hyperspace by the capital and up to four escorts: their jumps spool in a quarter of the time and they steer at the runner. A pursuer that comes within 40,000 units of a ship in warp pulls it (and the ships of its fleet that are in warp) out of hyperspace, as the Stop order does, and the fight goes on where it drops; it cannot start another jump for 30 days. A ship with a long enough head start still gets away, and the pursuit gives up after 50 days or when it is 4.5 sectors from where it began, stops at a system whose defence outweighs the pursuers if the runner got there first (a runner still in warp is pulled out short of it), is not made by a damaged unit (under 60 percent undamaged), by a capital that is besieging, landing, gathering, guarding the Beacon or answering a threat, or by a capital that has chased in the last 90 days. Your ships pulled out of warp get a message. New Beacon parameters: `beaconPursuitOn`, `beaconPursuitMax`, `beaconPursuitEscorts`, `beaconPursuitContactDays`, `beaconPursuitMaxSectors`, `beaconPursuitGiveUpDays`, `beaconPursuitCooldownDays`, `beaconPursuitPullRange`, `beaconPursuitSpeedPct`, `beaconPursuitSpoolPct`, `beaconPursuitMinHealthPct`, `beaconPursuitLockDays`, `beaconPursuitFightDays`, `beaconPursuitDefencePct`.
- **Destroying or boarding a Reclaimer cattle ship reveals the harvest core it served.** The ship's navigation record names the core: your first such kill always reveals one (with a pop-up), later kills that name a new core open another, and one naming a core you already know is a ticker line at most once a month. When every live core is known a final pop-up says so. Known cores get a small purple diamond over the world (system view) and its system (galaxy view); unknown cores look like any Reclaimer-held world, and the Intelligence list names only the ones you know. A known core that is destroyed or retaken loses its mark. AI empires learn cores the same way, and Smarter AI coalition members reach 50% further for a known core when retaking cores and go for it first.
- **The Reclaimers answer attacks and patrol their worlds.** Reserve fleets (one per eight held worlds, at least one) wait near the harvest cores; when a held world is invaded, blockaded or besieged, the nearest reserve that is strong enough to matter goes to defend it and returns when the threat is gone (no reserve goes to a hopeless fight, and every garrison fleet, Watcher fleet and the Beacon guard stay where they are, so a diversion can still pull the defence apart and the coalition can still retake worlds). Small patrol groups fly a route of nearby held worlds, a few days at each, engaging what they meet; a patrol that meets a superior force reports it, and a reserve may answer. They are paid from fleet biofuel: 12% (reserves) and 6% (patrols) of each season's budget is taken from the strike groups, so those are a little lighter. New options: beaconResponseOn, beaconResponseSharePct, beaconPatrolSharePct, beaconResponseWorldsPer, beaconPatrolWorldsPer and more (see the Giantworks add-on's settings).
- **Game Editor: Great Work: beacon (finished).** A new spawn entry places the Beacon already complete, with its Watchers mustered cloaked round it, so the ten-day countdown to the first mega beam starts at once.
- **Beacon cattle ships dock and unload visibly.** A laden cattle ship now sits at the harvester for 3 days before its people are penned (it used to unload the moment it arrived and, in saves from before the fleets, was scrapped at once, so it looked as if the ships vanished with their cargo). Old-save cattle ships beyond a core's pool size are retired after they unload instead of piling up idle. Only with the Beacon on.
- **The Beacon station is easier to break.** Its plating is halved (+300% armour, +200% shields instead of +600% / +400%), its guns lose their 1.5x boost, and its plates keep only a quarter of their reactive rating (only hits of 2.5 or less are shrugged off). Applies to Beacons built from now on; the reactive change also to existing ones. Every other Reclaimer ship's plates now cancel only hits up to 6 instead of 10.
- **Great Works start with the galaxy's tech and take 10 to 15 years.** Each Great Work now begins its Gathering when the empires' average tech reaches a level rolled between 3 and 4 (not before year 8; a fallback year of 40 to 60 keeps a slow galaxy from waiting for ever) and takes 10 to 15 years undisturbed instead of 5. The Watchers' corruption speeds the Beacon by 4% per world (at most 50%) instead of 9% (150%), and corrupted worlds no longer add extra deliveries on top of that, so a fully corrupted galaxy builds it in about 7 to 10 years.

## 0.1.17

### The Voidbloom War (new add-on in The Rim)

- An Opium-War-style arc around the Oranthi Concord. Voidbloom, a narcotic spore, grows only on deadly rim worlds worked
  by forced labour and held down by garrisons, and is smuggled into the Concord's closed market. Addiction spreads, the
  Concord's treasury bleeds and its commissioner cracks down with blockades, raids, burned fields, freed workers,
  freighter hunts and occupations. Tolerate it or go to war: a late war can end in an unequal treaty (ceded worlds,
  indemnities, treaty ports, legal Voidbloom), followed by one of five aftermaths. The Interstellar Diet can ban it,
  sanction either side or force the Concord's ports open.

### The Rim

- **A wild rim:** the AI rarely settles it, its planets are poorer, rim pirates are many and strong, and derelict
  high-tech warships wait to be claimed.
- **Concord patrols** around its colonies and stations, escorts for its construction ships, new listening posts.
- **Faster, deadlier creatures** that can catch ships, and about 2.5× more wildlife in the rim.
- **Rim Herders** earn money from their goods plus a small yearly stipend.
- **Ordinary empires no longer start inside the rim**, and that check no longer stalls "Founding empires".

### Galactic Threats

- **Generation Ships:** an early-game crisis. A world ship and a fleet about 1.3× its target's strength arrive at the
  galaxy edge, make contact and, 18 months later, demand a world: Pilgrims, Heirs, Ark Faith or the silent Quiet Ship.
- **Archive Cores:** lost tech to study (four yearly boosts) or seal for one large payoff.
- **The Dead Probe Swarm:** self-replicating machines that mine outward from their Mothercore and attack any ship in
  their systems, shown as a dark-brown presence on the map.

### Dark themes

- **Slavery:** penal colonies worked by enslaved peoples, with output up to 6× and tax up to 2× by staffing, at the price
  of garrisons, revolts and reputation; AI use depends on species and government.
- **Scorched earth** (Living Galaxy) and **War crimes remembered** (Politics & Characters) add-ons.

- **Deep (DW2-style) backdrop,** the new default for new games: the original image darkened, with slowly drifting rust and

- **Influence borders** (Advanced Display Settings): angular empire regions from colonies and stations, optional empire

### Multiplayer

- **Join a game in progress.** A new player can join a running LAN or Internet game: the host offers a free empire and
  you take control at the next frame.

  back. The last 100 chat lines are saved with the game.

  rejoin. Two-player games wait with Reconnect / Take Over instead of playing on alone.
- **Host on the Internet, join by code.** Invite and reply codes connect players directly (WebRTC, no server).
