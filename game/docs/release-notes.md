# Release notes

Notes for the next release, to copy into the GitHub release (RELEASING.md step 2).

## Unreleased

- **All Rim add-ons moved to Work in progress** (Rim Trade, Rim Fauna, Rim Herders, Rim Frontier, Rim Atmosphere): still being tuned.
- **Victory only from the Beacon.** No threat ending gives a victory screen any more except destroying the Beacon after it has fired its mega beam at least once (destroying it before the first launch does not end the game). Every other threat still ends or is contained with its messages and news, but the game goes on. Of the threat defeats only the Beacon's (80% of the galaxy's people gone) still ends the game; the Dark Farms, Robot Mutiny, Corporate Coup and Time Bomb defeats no longer do. The stock victory conditions and the Galactic Diet's emperor ending are unchanged.

- **Wreckage and Salvage retired, Surrender no longer leaves wrecks, The Silence disabled for now.** The Wreckage and Salvage add-on can no longer be switched on, and the Surrender gameplay change (formerly "Surrender and wrecks") drops its wrecks and debris fields (they showed raw text keys as names when the older add-on was off). The Silence is temporarily out of Galactic Threats and the Game Editor's spawn list; it will come back. Old saves load cleanly: their debris fields, wreck records and salvage runs are removed (salvage ships go idle), and The Silence's hyperdrive-blocking zone is lifted.
- **The Ancient Guardians and the Galactic Threats never interact (all but the Beacon).** The Purifier was destroyed by the Guardians, and the same now holds for the Cult, the Hive, the Grey Tide, the Hollow Fleet, the Dead Probe Swarm, the Generation Ships, the Robot Mutiny, the Doppelgangers, Dark Farms, the Ghost Armada, the Exchange, the Founders' Compact and the Ultramind, and the Voidbloom: their ships, bases, colonies and monsters never target, attack, blockade, invade, capture or damage the Guardians' (shots, missiles, torpedoes, area weapons, fighters, planet destroyers and ion cannons included), and the Guardians' ships and defences never target theirs; no war between them either way; no threat picks a Guardian world or empire as a victim, host or prey (infection, conversion, hacking, raids, risings); the Void Whale ignores them; the Silence's hyperdrive zone does not affect their ships. The Beacon's Reclaimers still fight them. A save where they were already fighting has the orders, targets and war cleared within five days. Only while the threat is active; nothing changes with the add-ons off.
- **Six new combat changes, now sub-options of the BVR combat add-on (Work in progress section of the add-on picker; on by default there, each switchable in the BVR combat settings panel; each also works without BVR combat).**
  - **Decoys and chaff.** A ship with a working countermeasures (ECM) component carries 3 decoy charges (one more per further ECM component, up to 2 more). When a missile or torpedo, BVR or stock, is in flight at it, it launches a decoy (at most one every 5 seconds): each round in flight that was going to hit has a 50% chance (torpedoes half that) to be pulled off target and veer away. Decoys are reloaded at your own or an ally's space ports and bases with a dock, or from a deployed resupply ship, for a few percent of the ECM component's price. The ship panel shows "Decoys 2/3". AI empires put at least one ECM component on every warship design and one more when their enemies field missiles.
  - **Ambush first strike.** A ship that opens fire (it has not fired for 20 seconds) on an empire that has not detected it, by the stock visibility rules, gets an ambush for 10 seconds: a shot that would miss hits instead 35% of the time and every hit does 50% more damage. In practice that means stealth ships, long-range fire from beyond a target's sensors in deep space, and attackers in systems the target cannot see. AI ships that are ambushing weigh the threats against them lighter. The ship panel shows the seconds left.
  - **Critical hits.** The first damage to an engine, a reactor or a command part (command centre, targeting computer) is critical 30% of the time: engines cut top speed by 30% a stack, a reactor makes every weapon fire 35% more slowly a stack, a command part turns 20% of that ship's hits into misses a stack; up to 3 stacks of each. The effect ends when the damaged part is repaired. The ship panel shows "Critical: engines, reactor". AI ships with critical damage weigh a losing fight 12% more heavily a stack, so crippled ships withdraw sooner.
  - **Damage control in battle.** In a fight crews repair one damaged component a minute for an average crew, checked every 3 game seconds (the stock self-repair of ships with a damage control component works as before); +20% a crew level above average, +25% a Veterans rank with that change on; engines, reactors and command parts first, which also ends their critical effects. A ship with over 70% of its parts damaged is past saving by its crew.
  - **Armour facing.** A hit on the bow does 25% less damage, on the side 10% more, on the stern 40% more (arcs of 60 degrees about the heading). Bases, blasts and chain explosions have no facing. AI fleets turn a ship that is holding still bow-on to the nearest enemy, and fast ships (30% or more faster than a moving target) circle to attack from the flank or stern instead of charging head-on.
  - **Surrender.** A badly crippled AI ship (under 45% of its components working) that cannot run (engines out, or something faster is shooting at it and it cannot jump away) and is outgunned (the hostile ships shooting at it have 1.5 times its working firepower) may strike its colours, checked after every hit that damages it and every 3 seconds, with a 30% chance scaled by its people's temper (cautious races give in sooner). It stops fighting and moving, leaves its fleet and is no longer shot at; an armed enemy that stays within 900 units for 20 seconds puts a prize crew aboard, using the original's boarding capture (the captor's capture policy decides whether it is kept, retrofitted or scrapped). If nobody comes in 120 game days the crew takes it back. Your own ships never surrender on their own. The ship panel shows "Struck colours".
  - All six come with BVR combat, are switched off one by one in its settings, are not in openDWU Classic (no add-on picker there), work with or without BVR combat, and every number is an add-on parameter. Saves round-trip their state; older saves load as before.
- **BVR combat: longer weapon ranges, double sensor ranges, shared sensors and a stand-off arrival (all BVR only, every number a parameter).** BVR missile launchers reach 3 times as far as before, torpedoes 1.25 times the original (not half, as before; their damage, speed and rate of fire are unchanged), lasers, phasers and other beams and rail guns 1.5 times, ion weapons 1.25 times and point defence 1.5 times, for ships, bases and planet defences alike; area and wave weapons, tractor and gravity beams, planet destroyers, fighters and bombers are unchanged. Long-range scanners, proximity arrays and trace scanners reach twice as far, and the missile lock range doubles (4800 plus a tenth of the scanner range). Sensors are shared: a ship may fire on a target that any friendly ship of its empire (or a mutual-defence ally) within 9600 units of it tracks, whether in its fleet or not. A fleet with loaded missile launchers that is ordered to attack or invade in another system gathers at 80% of its missile range from the target, instead of at the target, and opens with salvos before closing.
- **BVR combat (new add-on in the picker's Work in progress section, off by default; no longer a Gameplay changes checkbox, and not in openDWU Classic). The six combat changes below are its sub-options; saves and old settings with any of them on load with the matching options on, and the Gameplay changes panel is now two rows:** fights open at missile range. A new missile launcher family (Javelin, Longbow, Stormspear, Skyfire: four tiers and three seeker upgrades on a new row of the weapons tree) fires very fast, very accurate missiles from 1,800 to 3,600 units; each launcher holds 4 missiles and four hits kill a frigate of the matching era. The stock torpedoes become close-range, slow, heavy hitters with 6 torpedoes a launcher (two hits kill a frigate of their era). Missiles need a sensor lock (stealth and jamming shorten it, long-range scanners and proximity arrays extend it) and ECM spoils it; point defence shoots missiles and torpedoes down, and a ship with point defence covers its fleet, so escorts matter. Magazines show on the ship panel ("Missiles 3/8, Torpedoes 12/12") and in the ship designer's component stats, and are reloaded for credits at your own or allied space ports and bases with a dock, or from deployed resupply ships (slower); a ship on a Refuel order waits until it is full. AI empires research and design launchers and torpedoes, fit more point defence against missile fleets, open with missile salvos or keep their distance when they out-range the enemy, rush when out-ranged, rearm after a fight (your automated fleets too) and count a half-empty ship as weaker. While it is on, AI warships are designed by its doctrine instead of Smarter AI's design options (which assume beams and torpedoes). Every number is an add-on parameter.
- **Giantworks: every finished Great Work but the Beacon can be taken by ground war.** Its base carries about 30,000 shields, which refill from empty over about a year and not at all under fire; once they are shot down that year is the window to land troops on it (order troop ships to attack it, or select it and choose Invade). While its shields are down only 40% of its guns fire, sparing troop carriers while any other hostile ship is near; with half its parts damaged they fall silent. A garrison of 6 to 14 troops (by wonder) holds it, and the fight is the stock ground war in the stock Ground Report ("[" or its Ground Report button). Win and the wonder and its benefits are yours: no war crime, but every empire thinks less of you. Smarter AI empires go for the wonders they value, land their troops once the shields are down and, when the guns fall silent, invade or destroy by their temper, the wonder's worth and their troops in reach. The Ark Engine now flees only once when its shields break and can be taken at its escape system while it mends. All of it is part of the Giantworks add-on (off by default); its numbers are in the add-on settings.
- **Giantworks: the Ark Engine has its own art:** a planet-sized globe ship (wider than the largest gas giant) kitbashed from stock station and capital-ship hulls in their own painted metal: great hull plates, a broad equatorial band of decks and engine bells, a habitation ring on six spokes that turns slowly (passing behind the globe like a planet's ring), and a docking iris in the band. It stays upright like a planet; the engines on its trailing side glow and throw plumes as it moves. Siphoning, the iris opens toward the colony with a tractor beam and a stream of passenger ships funnelling in; attacked, its turrets fire, warning strobes flash and its shield shimmers where it is hit; with its shields broken it burns and smokes, its band lights failing in sections. It is drawn under the ships, so fleets fighting it fly over it.
- **Giantworks, the Beacon: the harvest economy.** The Reclaimers no longer drain a world where they took it. Once they hold worlds they make the most populous of them **harvest cores**, spread across their holdings: 2 in a galaxy of under 700 stars, 3 under 1,400, from 4 rising to 7 in the biggest galaxies (7 at 4,000 stars and more), never more than one per three worlds they hold. A **harvester**, a plated machine station, orbits each core and takes about four months to build. Every other world they hold launches a huge boxy **cattle ship** every 20 days with a twentieth of its people (they leave the world at the launch) to the nearest core, where they wait in its pens; each month a core renders a fifth of its pens, and slowly its own people, into **biofuel** (6% of the three strongest empires' military in warship strength per billion people, `beaconFuelPerBillionPct`). The processed are eliminated (the 80% loss measure). Biofuel pays for their troops (Thrall regiments raised at the nearest core and flown to the landings in transports you can intercept; the garrisons of their worlds, threatened worlds first, and a garrison that has grown far past its size is sent home) and, by the new setting `beaconFundingModel`, their fleets: 0 = only the harvest (an awakening reserve starts the first season; cut the harvest and they starve), 1 = the old scripted growth with the biofuel buying troops, 2 = a guaranteed trickle of half the scripted strength with the biofuel on top, at most 1.5 times the scripted season (the default). The scripted season strength is lower than before (15% of the three strongest empires' military per strike group, +2% a season, +1% per held world), since the waves now really reach it. The Beacon's own guard is paid from the same fuel. **Cutting the harvest:** a cattle ship spools its drive twelve times as long as a warship and crawls in to its core, so it can be caught; destroying it kills its captives (no reputation is lost for it, and by default they do not count toward the Reclaimers' 80%: setting `beaconCattleDeadCountPct`); boarding and taking it frees them. Retaking a core, or destroying its harvester, stops it and frees everyone in its pens, who return as population to the liberator's nearest worlds; another world becomes the core (one whose harvester was destroyed only after a year). The AI coalition now intercepts cattle ships and gathers to strike harvesters, and Smarter AI empires make the cores war objectives and send invasion fleets to retake them. Also: the Reclaimers' waves now spawn by the time since the last pass (the scenario pass runs about every 36 days, so a season used to field only two or three batches of ships whatever its strength); a Watcher taken by another empire is lost to the faction at once; a new Galactopedia article, "The Beacon and the Reclaimers". Art keys for the new ships: `cattle` (the cattle ship, a large freighter hull grown by passenger holds) and `harvester`.
- **Giantworks: the Ultramind is reworked.** It keeps a *standing* for each contract partner instead of one shared mood: a war between partners costs only the empire that declared it (the one attacked loses nothing), and attacks on it, sabotage, being boarded and refused requests cost only whoever did them; everyone drifts down slowly, the holder (the empire whose territory holds it) faster, faster still while it refuses the Ultramind's requests. Every partner is now asked for favours (the holder twice as often), and a request can be a warfleet to study. Research is **+100% for the holder and +50% for the other partners**. When any partner's standing reaches nothing it wakes and goes to war **only with the partners whose standing had collapsed** (and with whoever attacks it later); the partners in good standing stay neutral and are offered a deal in a pop-up (keep the contract at half the bonus and put trade sanctions on the offenders, or decline); hacking, dark ports and stopped freight hit only the empires it is at war with. New ways to protect yourself: **withdraw** from the contract at any time (a threat action on its station), and a **Diet motion to sanction the empire upsetting it** (trade sanctions and the end of its contract); AI partners deploy a Logic Bomb or withdraw when their own standing is dangerously low. The holder sets one **directive** at a time, with action buttons under the selection panel (and in the right-click menu) when the station is selected, a Directive line in the panel showing the current one: Research (a project completed outright now and then, on top of the bonus, which is a third under the other directives), War (targeting and countermeasures for warships near it, early warning of hostile warships), Economy (tax and trade income, faster construction, cheaper ships, lower upkeep) or Intelligence (hidden threats confirmed, stronger counter-intelligence, spies caught, free maps of rivals); switching takes 60 days, the partners get half of every effect, and its requests match the directive. A finished Ultramind built round a planet now shows, about monthly (the same for every player, nothing saved), either as a dense machine cage round the planet or as the solid cube drawn over it (sized to cover the planet, however big a gas giant).

Planet and moon sizes are unchanged (they never grew).

- **The Beacon (new Great Work wonder for the Giantworks add-on, flag gwBeacon; off unless chosen):** one of the things the independents' secret Great Work can turn out to be (only rolled with six or more empires). *Before it is finished*, two Watchers, huge cloaked machine ships, wait in dark space beyond the galaxy rim. Every few months a Watcher drifts over a colony that was independent at the start and CORRUPTS it (as the Cult converts: the governor and people quietly serve the Beacon, even when an empire owns the world); each corrupted world speeds the build up (+9% each, up to +150%) and sends freighters to the site. Now and then a Watcher drops its cloak over a colony for a few days (a news line and a message to the owner: "a vast unidentified vessel"), passive and never targeted by the AI, then slips away; it fights only if attacked (then it is at locked war with the attacker). Watchers are enormous (about 60 times the armour and 9 times the shields of a capital ship) and very hard to kill; killing one slows the build to 40% (both: 16%). A ship or base of an empire close to a cloaked Watcher may spot it (3% a check): the cloak then lapses for that empire only and it shows in Threats & Leads. *When it is finished* the machine faction becomes the Reclaimers, at locked war with every empire, and wages campaign SEASONS. PREPARATION (4-6 months): waves spawn at the Beacon and fly to 2-3 deep-space STAGING POINTS, one strike group per target world (spread over different empires); any empire whose sensors reach a staging fleet sees it on the map and in Threats & Leads, and hitting it thins the season. THE OFFENSIVE: every strike group leaves ON THE SAME DAY for its own target (travel takes 3 to 12 months); a group lands its troops (all at once, the stock ground war decides) only once its escort holds the orbit and the troops beat the defence by 150%; a failed landing raises the margin and brings reinforcing transports; corrupted worlds rise from inside at the start of the offensive; after a long failed siege of a world they cannot take they rarely (30%) send a planet destroyer. About a year of QUIET follows, with only raids by pairs of ships. PROCESSING: a conquered world's people are drained over about 8 months into Thrall regiments that Reclaimer transports carry to the next world, and each processing world makes the next season stronger; retaking a world stops the drain and saves the survivors. The game is LOST when the Reclaimers have eliminated 80% of the galaxy's people (drained or destroyed, against the population when the Beacon was lit). Counterplay with no new screens: race the processing, retake or bombard processing worlds, intercept Thrall transports (they are real ships), hit the staging points, salvage (every Reclaimer ship you destroy, and every hull you board, earns points toward four counter-tech steps: free shields breakthrough, free weapons and armour breakthroughs, corruption resistance, and the Beacon's signal traced), counter-intelligence finds corrupted worlds (then purge them for 250,000, a threat action), and the DARK BARGAIN: a Watcher privately offers an empire the Reclaimers have hurt a deal through a decision pop-up, either give up a colony and be spared for 2 years or become their protectorate and fight on their side until they liquify it after 8 years; both are secret until another empire finds out, and then it is a war crime ("collaboration", tried by the Diet when it runs). Victory: the Beacon destroyed, both Watchers destroyed and no world held by the Reclaimers. They are a crisis faction for the crisis interactions (reputation, opportunists, the council). Game Editor: "Great Work: beacon" starts the Great Work as the Beacon at once. Its hints are the cargoes superconductors, navigation beacons and black alloys with cloaking mesh.

A cloaked Watcher can no longer be ordered at by any empire (the Ancient Guardians kept attacking and finally boarded one), or shot at or boarded by the stock combat unless the empire spotted it or it fired first; ships ordered at it while it was in view lose track when it slips out of sight; a Watcher taken by another empire counts as lost to the faction. Staging fleets lie in deep space where no sensor reaches, so they were never seen: an empire with a colony within five sectors of the point now notices the gathering (25% per five days at its door, falling to nothing at the range). Strength is now per strike group (20% of the three strongest empires' military each in season 1, +3% per season), and the number of groups grows with the galaxy (3, one more every two seasons, one more per 20 colonies, at most 16), so a bigger galaxy meets a bigger campaign; the Beacon's own guard is two groups' worth. A planet destroyer sent after a long failed siege is kept on its Attack order (before, it was sent once and forgotten). Only one Beacon can exist per galaxy. **The empires answer together** (new `beaconAi.ts`, AI empires only, a human is never ordered): every AI empire at war with the Reclaimers pools what any of them knows and gives up to 60% of its military to the coalition: relief fleets Patrol a world a strike group is bound for; a staging fleet any of them noticed is struck by the fleets that can gather at a waypoint short of it in time, all at once; the Beacon is assaulted once a force of 150% of its guard has gathered; a Watcher whose cloak lapsed for any of them is hunted. Units not ordered at the next pass are released to the stock AI. Soak-tested over 8 to 15 years on 400 to 1400 stars with 7 to 20 empires (tracked in game/scripts/beacon-soak-hook.mjs).

- **Soak-test fixes (threats, crisis interactions, Hive, Cult).** (1) *Contained before it began is not a victory:* a Cult whose converts were all purged before the theocracy rose, a Robot Mutiny whose transmitter was taken before it triggered, and a Grey Tide whose nests were all destroyed before it declared now end only their own story (the news is posted); the game no longer ends with a Victory. A threat that rose and was then destroyed still ends it. (2) *The Hive:* its nodes are now every colony that was independent, including the ones an older (late-start) galaxy had already settled into an empire, recorded from game start whenever the add-on is on (also in a game where the Hive rolled \"not this game\", so a Game Editor spawn knows them). Reaching the absorption threshold no longer raises the Chorus at once: from then on it has a 20% chance each game year (hiveRiseChancePct). Each absorbed node now rises with troops 6 times its colony's defence, up to 3,000,000 (hiveRisingOverkill, hiveRisingMaxStrength), so the Chorus really takes them; 0 gives the old militia-only risings. (3) *The Cult and the Ancient Guardians:* the creed never converts, spreads to, rises in or takes anything of the Ancient Guardians (no war with them either way). When the Cult or the Hive wipes out an empire it is handed its strongest 10 warships only (cultCapturedFleetMax, hiveCapturedFleetMax); the rest are scuttled. (4) *Crisis interactions:* killing pirate ships, or being handed a colony by agreement (Generation Ships' cession), no longer counts as harming the galaxy (the Diet no longer tabled a peaceful settled faction as a threat, with a joint war); a crisis that has ended (the Purifier destroyed) no longer marks empires down for standing aside, though its killer is still credited. (5) *Coordinated landings:* a hold left on a re-tasked transport no longer counts as a hold. (6) *Fixes:* an empire with no capital is skipped when looking for an ally against an enemy (a threat faction has none until it owns a colony; it threw out of the empire's tick), a dead character that gets a skill/trait message no longer throws out of the intelligence missions, and Refugees & Demographics saves its dominant-race and tension-warning bookkeeping.
- **The Purifier: war-forged escorts and a ruined dock.** The Purifier's escorts and guards (escort, frigate, destroyer, cruiser, capital ship, carrier, troop transport) have new art in the planet destroyer's own colours (silver and champagne plating, pale-gold and copper trim, dark steel machinery), so they read as its fleet. The finished Beacon is now a massive relay in the Mass Effect style, assembled from the same mod's Reaper parts: a great plated ring housing its core, two long parallel prongs of stacked hull segments reaching out of one side, antenna masts on its back, with wine-red seam lines and deep teal markings. In its core three gyroscope rings turn all the time, faster and faster as the Reclaimers' preparation counts down to the next offensive (a blur on its eve), and the core heats from a dim wine red through orange to white-hot; it winds down and cools after the offensive. On launch day, when the strike groups are called out, every human player's camera is sent to the Beacon (the game is not paused; Escape returns to where you were, and any camera input hands control back): charge pulses race down both prongs into the core, and a blinding beam fires straight up out of it, a swirling white vortex with a green rim, rings rising, hundreds of fine dark embers with white-hot cores thrown out of its base, to a long, deep blast of our own synthesis (at the Effects volume). The flash is capped so it never strobes. A minute after the first launch an event appears: "The End Times". Damage shows on the station: light damage sparks and flickers; heavy damage pits the plating, snaps and bends a prong, sheds debris and smoke; near death the core sputters and the gyros stutter.
